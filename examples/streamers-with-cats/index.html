Two streamers, two cats, baby on the way

We are
couple, 2 cats, we both game and stream
Coming up
a baby in about 2 years
Must-haves
a gaming room for two, cat-safe windows

Claude’s answer

Best fit: Layout 1, "Studio in Bedroom 3", and no walls come down. Bedroom 3 becomes your gaming room for two now, and it never has to move. Bedroom 2 is a guest and cat room until the baby comes, then it becomes the nursery next to your room. With the baby, you'll need all three bedrooms, so knocking down walls doesn't gain you much.

Wall check

Walls I'm not sure about: - W1 sits directly under the structural wall S1. Check for a beam above it, measure its thickness and do a knock test. - W2, W4, W6 (the bedroom fronts and the main bedroom door wall) probably have light switches and trunking inside them. - W7 (kitchen to yard) is drawn with a grey fill, so ask the contractor what it's made of.

Layouts

1. Studio in Bedroom 3 (best fit): now, then after the baby - Nothing is hacked. Bedroom 3 has two desks back to back with a 1.5 m shelf between them, plus tall storage on the wall it shares with Bedroom 2. - Now: Bedroom 2 is a guest and cat room, with a sofa bed, a cat tree at the window and a wardrobe. After: it becomes the nursery, with the cot on the wall next to your room, a changing table and a glider. The wardrobe stays. - Pros: nothing to hack or undo, the nursery is right next to you, and the gaming room never moves. - Cons: the gaming room shares a wall with the nursery, so the storage wall and door seals have to work.

2. Gaming suite in the main bedroom (after) - Nothing is hacked. Now: Bedroom 2 is the guest and cat room. - The main bedroom has the desks side by side under the window, a couch and TV for co-op, and its own bathroom. You sleep in Bedroom 3, and the nursery is between you. - Pros: the biggest room, with a bathroom for long sessions. - Cons: you give up the en-suite, it still shares a wall with the nursery, and side-by-side mics pick each other up.

3. Baby in your room first (after) - Nothing is hacked. Now: the same as Layout 1. - The cot sits in your room for the first year. Bedroom 2 stays a guest room with a single bed, for a confinement nanny or grandparents. - Pros: easy night feeds, and space for someone to help. Cons: your room gets tight, and you'll still convert Bedroom 2 later.

4. Glass wall to the living room (after) - W1 comes down and is replaced by fixed glass. Now: Bedroom 2 is the guest and cat room. - The desks sit side by side for co-op, with a loveseat behind them. The play mat sits just outside the glass, so you can watch the baby and the cats from your desk. - Pros: more light and a view of the living room. Cons: a permit, hacking and glass all cost money, glass lets more sound through, and the living room shows behind you on stream (fit blinds).

Gaming room

  • Two streamers in one room: sitting back to back with the shelf between you gives each of you a backdrop, and your mics point away from each other. Use dynamic or cardioid mics.
  • Noise: acoustic panels stop echo, not noise. Put the tall storage on the nursery wall and fit door seals.
  • Wiring: two PCs make a lot of heat, so give Bedroom 3 its own aircon. The A/C ledge is at the far end of the flat, so the trunking run to Bedroom 3 is the longest one. Plan sockets, LAN cables and the trunking before the carpentry is built.

Cats and cat-safe windows

  • Fit mesh to every window that opens. The most important one is the yard opening to the drying rack, which has no window. The bathroom windows open onto the drying rack and the A/C ledge, so mesh those too. Invisible grilles alone leave gaps a cat can squeeze through. Grilles at the service yard need an HDB-approved design.
  • Put a mesh screen on the main gate, because cats tend to slip out when the door opens.
  • Put both litter boxes in the yard, and keep the yard door ajar or fit a cat flap. Put cat trees by meshed windows.
  • Two cats is HDB's limit. Since 1 Sep 2026, keeping a cat that isn't microchipped and licensed with AVS is an offence. AVS also expects owners to fit mesh or grilles so cats can't fall.
  • Later, for the baby: add window locks in the nursery. A baby gate with a cat door at K1 keeps the toddler out of the kitchen but still lets the cats reach their litter.

Check with HDB or your contractor before any hacking

  • Permit and engineer: you need a permit through an HDB-registered contractor, plus a Professional Engineer if W1 turns out to be reinforced concrete.
  • Completion date: when was the block completed? That sets the 3-year bathroom rule. None of these layouts touch the bathrooms.
  • Services: switches, pipes and trunking inside W1, W2, W4 and W6.
  • Beams: any beam above W1. If W1 goes, expect a bulkhead line on the ceiling.
  • Site measurements: measure on site before you order furniture.

I worked from a photo of your plan, and the photo is slightly skewed, so treat sizes as accurate to about 100–150 mm and check them against your official HDB plan.
