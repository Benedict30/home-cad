Couple with a Shih Tzu, no kids

We are
couple, no kids, 1 Shih Tzu
Coming up
more work from home
Must-haves
a dog wash spot, easy-to-clean floors

Claude’s answer

Best fit: Layout A, two separate offices with nothing hacked. Bedroom 3 becomes the study now. When you're both working from home more, Bedroom 2 becomes the second office, with a sofa bed for guests, so you can each close a door for calls. The yard gets a raised dog wash tub, and one waterproof, scratch-resistant floor runs through the flat.

Unsure: W1 carries on from the black wall, so it may be reinforced concrete. W3 and W5 may sit under beams at the facade. W2, W6 and W7 may hide switches or pipes. Check these with your contractor's permit application to HDB, by measuring thickness and doing a knock test, and by looking for services and beams.

Layouts

A. Two offices, keep every wall (best fit) - Now: Bedroom 3 is the study (desk at the window, bookshelf behind you for calls, dog bed). Bedroom 2 is the guest room (queen bed, wardrobe). - After: Bedroom 2 becomes Office 2 (desk at the window, sofa bed). Only the furniture changes. - Pros: a door each for calls, fully reversible. Cons: once you're both home, guests get a sofa bed.

B. Shared office + guest room (no hacking) - Now: same as A. After: both desks go in Bedroom 3, at right angles with a divider shelf between them. - Pros: Bedroom 2 stays a proper guest room for visiting family. Cons: two people on calls in one room, so you'll need headsets.

C. Open den (W1 and W2 come down) - Now: Bedroom 3 is a den with a shared work table, a daybed and the dog's play mat. After: Bedroom 2 becomes the call office. - Pros: a bigger, brighter living area, and the dog plays near you. Cons: one quiet room, one bedroom fewer, and rebuilding the walls costs money.

D. Glass study + glass kitchen (W1 swapped for glass) - Now: a glass study plus the guest room. After: as A. Glass sliding doors on the kitchen edge replace the pet gates. - Pros: the study borrows light, and the dog stays out of the kitchen without gates. Cons: costs more, and glass shows nose prints.

Your must-haves

  • Dog wash: a raised tub in the yard (about 850 mm high) beside the washer, with a handheld shower, hot water and a non-slip mat. Check the yard's floor trap and tap positions with your plumber.
  • Floors:
  • Living room, corridor and bedrooms: one waterproof SPC vinyl with a matte, textured finish. It has no grout lines, resists claws and gives small paws grip.
  • Kitchen and yard: matte porcelain tiles, with epoxy grout in the yard.
  • Avoid high-gloss tiles and real wood.
  • Leave the bathroom tiles alone (3-year rule) and add an anti-slip treatment instead.
  • A Shih Tzu is on HDB's approved list (1 dog per flat), so you're fine.
  • In every layout:
  • a shoe cabinet with a seat, plus a leash hook, by the door
  • the shelter as a storeroom with free-standing racks for dog supplies
  • a utility cabinet in the yard
  • aircon trunking for both offices, planned before the carpentry goes in

Check with HDB or your contractor before any hacking

  • The permit, through an HDB-registered contractor, and a Professional Engineer if a wall turns out to be reinforced concrete
  • Your block's completion date, which decides whether the bathroom rule still applies
  • Switches, pipes and trunking inside W1, W2 and W7
  • Beams above W2–W5
  • On-site measurements: usable space is about 100–200 mm smaller than these centre-line sizes

I read all this from a photo of your plan taken at a slight angle, so please check it against the official HDB plan.
