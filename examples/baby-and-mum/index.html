Couple working from home, baby on the way

We are
couple, both work from home
Coming up
baby next year, my mum may move in
Must-haves
a proper home office

Claude’s answer

Best fit: Layout 1, rotate the rooms. You each get your own office now. When the baby and your mum arrive, Mum takes Bedroom 3, the cot stays in your room for a year or two, and Bedroom 2 becomes a shared office. Nothing gets knocked down, because you'll need all three bedrooms.

What I read: this is a 4-room flat of about 93 m². Sizes are to wall centre-lines, so rooms are about 100–200 mm smaller inside.

Room Size
Living/dining 3.55 × 6.8 m plus a dining bay
Main bedroom 3.0 × 4.8 m, with its own bathroom (Bath 1)
Bedroom 3 3.15 × 3.7 m
Bedroom 2 2.9 × 3.7 m, door facing the common bathroom (Bath 2)
Kitchen about 3.7 × 2.6 m, open to the dining area

Wall check

Wall Can it go? Why
S1, S2 No Structural
H (shelter) No Shelter rules: only small screw fixings
B1–B4 (bathrooms) Not for 3 years after the block's completion. Limited after that, with a permit Bathroom rules
E (external walls, windows, main door) No External
W1–W7 (bedroom, corridor and kitchen/yard partitions) Yes, with HDB approval Thin partitions
K (kitchen edge) There's no wall here You can add glass doors

I'm not sure about three of the thin walls. W1 sits under S1 and may be concrete. W3/W5 may have beams above them. W7 may have pipes inside. Your contractor's permit application to HDB will confirm them, along with a thickness and knock test and a check for switches, trunking and beams.

Layouts (no walls come down in any of them)

1. Rotate the rooms (best fit) - Now: Bed 2 is Office A. Bed 3 is Office B, with a 1900×900 sofa bed for guests. - After: - A 1300×700 cot sits beside your 1600×2000 queen bed. - Bed 2 becomes a shared office: two desks facing away from each other (1500×600 and 1600×600) with a 1600×400 bookshelf between them. - Bed 3 becomes Mum's room: a 1070×2000 super single bed and a clear 1.5 m turning circle. - Pro: you can take calls in separate rooms now, and you keep a closed-door office through the baby years. - Con: later you'll both take calls in one 2.8 m room. Around age 2 your child needs Bed 2, and the office moves to the Layout 2 study.

2. Glass study in the living room - Now: a glass study at the living-room window, with two 1300×600 desks back to back. Bed 2 and Bed 3 are guest rooms. - After: Bed 2 becomes the nursery, next to your room. Bed 3 becomes Mum's room. - Pro: every room has its final use from day one. - Con: a smaller living room (1900×900 sofa, 1400×800 dining table), and the study is noisier.

3. Mum opposite the bathroom - Now: Bed 3 is a shared office. Bed 2 is a guest room. - After: Bed 2 becomes Mum's room. The cot goes in your room. - Pro: Mum has the shortest walk to the bathroom at night, and the office is away from the baby's naps. - Con: Mum gets the smaller room, and your child's room won't be next to yours later.

4. If Mum doesn't move in - After: Bed 2 is the nursery. Bed 3 is a shared office for good. - Pro: a permanent office. - Con: no guest room.

In every layout

  • At the door: a 1700×400 shoe cabinet with a seat, and a 900×600 stroller bay.
  • Storage: the shelter becomes a storeroom with free-standing racks, and the yard gets a utility cabinet.
  • For the baby: a play mat instead of a coffee table, and window grilles or locks.
  • For Mum: night lights, no rugs and 900 mm clear doorways. HDB's EASE programme subsidises grab bars and a foldable shower seat in Bath 2 if she's 65 or older, or 60–64 and needs help with daily activities.
  • Before carpentry: plan the aircon trunking. Add glass kitchen doors if you cook with a wok.

Before any hacking, check with HDB or your contractor

  • The permit, and whether a Professional Engineer is needed for reinforced-concrete walls
  • Your block's completion date (when was it?)
  • Services inside the walls, and beams above them
  • Measurements taken on site

I read all of this from a photo, so check it against the official plan.
