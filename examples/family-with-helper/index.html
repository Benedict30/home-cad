Family of five with a live-in helper

We are
couple with 3 kids (ages 2, 6 and 9) and a live-in helper
Coming up
the older two will need proper study space
Must-haves
the helper gets her own room

Claude’s answer

Best fit: Layout A now, then Layout B once your youngest is ready to share a room (around 3–4). No walls come down in either. You need all three bedrooms: one for you, one for your helper and one for the kids. In B the three kids get the biggest room, with a desk for two and their own bathroom.

Layouts (no walls removed in any of them)

A · Now (recommended): bunk room in Bedroom 3 - Bedroom 3: your 6- and 9-year-old, with a bunk bed and a desk for two under the window. Bedroom 2: helper. Main bedroom: you, plus the toddler's cot. - Pros: works today with no building work, and both older kids get a proper desk spot. - Cons: the room is full. When the toddler moves in, switch to B.

B · After (recommended): kids take the main bedroom - Main bedroom: all three kids (bunk bed, single bed, desk for two, wardrobe) with Bath 1. Bedroom 3: you (queen bed). Bedroom 2: helper. - Pros: the most space for the most people, it's only a furniture move, and there's room for a bookshelf divider if the kids need privacy as teens. - Cons: you lose the ensuite and share Bath 2 with your helper; queen bed, not king.

C · Sleep room + homework wall - Bedroom 3: all three kids sleep (bunk bed + toddler bed). A desk for two goes on the living-room window wall. You keep the main bedroom. - Pros: homework doesn't wake the toddler, and you can see the desks from the kitchen. - Cons: noise from the TV and dinner during homework, and the living room loses a wall.

D · Kids next to you - Bedroom 2: the older two (bunk bed + desk for two). Bedroom 3: helper, on the living-room side. Main bedroom: you + cot. - Pros: the kids' room shares a wall with yours, and everyone gets more privacy. - Cons: the kids get the smaller room and a shorter desk.

In every layout - Your helper gets a proper bed, a window, a wardrobe and a door that closes. Her own room also covers MOM's rule that she can't share a room with an adult or teenage male. The shelter isn't a bedroom. - Shelter as a storeroom with free-standing racks. - A long shoe cabinet with a seat at the door, plus a spot for the stroller. - A play mat instead of a coffee table, and window grilles or locks throughout. - A 6-seat dining table. - A utility cabinet and washer-dryer in the yard. - Plan the aircon trunking before any carpentry. - If you cook with a wok, add a glass sliding panel along the open kitchen edge.

Before any hacking, confirm with HDB or your contractor - Permits go through an HDB-registered (DRC) contractor, and you need a Professional Engineer for any concrete wall. - Your block's completion date (the 3-year bathroom rule). - Switches, pipes and trunking inside the walls. - Beams above any wall you'd move. - Measurements taken on site before you order the bunk, the desks and the wardrobes.

I read all this from a photo of your floor plan, so check the sizes against the official HDB plan.
