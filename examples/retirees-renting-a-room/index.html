Retired couple renting out a room

We are
retired couple in our 60s with a small dog
Coming up
we want to rent out one room for extra income
Must-haves
a hobby room for painting, and a flat that’s easy to get around as we age

Claude’s answer

Best fit: Layout A. Rent out Bedroom 3, make Bedroom 2 your studio, and keep the master for the two of you. No walls come down. The tenant gets the room nearest the front door and the common bathroom. The studio sits between their room and yours, and your own bathroom is a few steps from your bed.

All four layouts are drawn to scale in the plans, each with a Now and an After renting view.

Wall check

Wall Can it go? Why
S1 Living / Bed 3, upper part No Structural (shaded black on the plan)
S2 Shelter No Its walls, door, floor and ceiling all stay
B1–B3 Bathroom walls No Not in the first 3 years after the block is completed. After that, only limited changes, with a permit
E External walls, windows, main door wall No Must stay
W1 Living / Bed 3, lower part Yes, with approval. Keep it Bedroom 3 has to stay a bedroom
W2, W4 Bedrooms / corridor Yes, with approval. Keep them A rented room has to stay an original bedroom
W3, W5 Between the bedrooms Yes, with approval. Keep them You need all three bedrooms
W6 Kitchen / yard Yes, with approval. Keep it Keeps the laundry and dog washing in their own wet area

Not sure yet: - W1 joins the structural wall above it and could be concrete. Check its thickness and do a knock test. - W6 may carry the washer pipes. - The raised bit on the bath wall by the master door is probably a pipe duct. - There may be beams over W2–W5.

Your contractor's permit application to HDB will settle each of these.

Four layouts (none removes a wall, because you need every bedroom)

A. Tenant up front ★ - Master: you · Bed 2: studio · Bed 3: guest room now, rented later (add a lock) - ✓ The tenant is nearest the door, the studio sits between you, and you keep your own bathroom - ✗ The tenant's room backs onto the living room, so the TV goes on the far window wall

B. Bigger studio - Master: you · Bed 3: studio · Bed 2: guest room now, rented later - ✓ About 10% more studio space, with room for a sofa bed and a plan chest - ✗ The tenant sleeps on the other side of your bedroom wall, and the smaller room earns less rent

C. Rent the master suite - Now: the same as A. After: the tenant takes the master and Bath 1, you move to Bed 3, and Bath 2 is yours alone - ✓ The highest rent, no shared bathroom, and the studio sits between you - ✗ You give up the biggest room and your own bathroom, with only 0.6–0.8 m beside your bed

D. Studio in the living room - Bed 3: studio now, rented later · Bed 2: guest room · after renting, the easel moves to the 3 m living-room window - ✓ Keeps a proper guest room for family, and you paint in the best daylight - ✗ Paint, smells and clutter end up in the space you share with the tenant

Built into every layout - Getting around: 1.5 m turning space in the living room and your bedroom. Night lights from your bed to the bathroom and along the corridor. A shoe bench at the door, seats with armrests, and no rugs. - EASE: if one of you is 65 or older (or 60–64 and needs help with daily activities), HDB subsidises grab bars, slip-resistant treatment, a fold-down shower seat and ramps. - Dog: slip-resistant, scratch-resistant flooring suits all three of you. A top-hung glass slider at the open kitchen keeps the dog out and wok smoke in, with no gate or floor track to trip on. A waist-height wash tub goes in the yard. HDB allows one dog of an approved breed, so no second dog. - Renting: needs HDB approval. A 4-room flat can rent out up to 2 original bedrooms, and occupancy is capped at 8 until the end of 2028, then 6 (you'd be 3). Check the minimum occupation period rules for your flat, and tell tenants about the dog. These are HDB's rules as I know them, so confirm on HDB's site before you apply. - Painting: with a tenant and a dog at home, use acrylics or water-mixable oils. Wash brushes in the yard tub, and keep canvases (not solvents) on the shelter racks. - Later: if you ever need a live-in carer, the rented room becomes her room, one more reason to keep every bedroom wall. The bedroom doors look about 850 mm wide; widening them to 900 mm clear for a wheelchair means cutting into W2/W4.

Before any hacking or door-widening, check with HDB or your contractor - Whether you need a permit, and a Professional Engineer for concrete walls - Your block's completion date, for the 3-year bathroom rule - Switches, pipes and trunking inside the wall - Beams above it - Measurements taken on site. I've read these sizes from a photo of the plan, so check everything against your official HDB plan.
