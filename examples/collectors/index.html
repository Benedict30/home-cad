Claude’s answer

Best fit: Layout 1, Gallery + walk-in suite. Bedroom 3 becomes the display room, with a see-through glass cabinet facing the sofa. Bedroom 2 becomes a walk-in wardrobe with a sneaker wall, opening off the main bedroom's entrance. Only two short partitions come down, and space for the collection to grow is planned in from day one.

Walls I'm unsure about: - W3 could be reinforced concrete, and removing it would then need a Professional Engineer (PE). - W1 lines up with column S3, so expect a beam above it.

To check: look at your contractor's permit application to HDB, measure the wall's thickness and do a knock test, look for switches, pipes and trunking inside, and look for a beam above.

Layouts

1. Gallery + walk-in suite ★ (W1 and W5 come down) - Bedroom 3 → display room: LEGO cabinets on both long walls, a Bearbrick wall and a build table at the window. A double-sided glass cabinet fills the W1 gap. - Bedroom 2 → walk-in wardrobe: 2.5 m of wardrobe, a 2.5 m sneaker wall and a dehumidifier. Its corridor door stays. - Room to grow: a 250 mm gallery shelf along the corridor (800 mm left to walk), a display tower by the dining table, and the shelter as the box archive. - ✅ The collection is on show but dust-sealed, and Bedroom 2 could later become a nursery off the main bedroom. ⚠️ Needs a hacking permit, and only one room stays a bedroom.

2. Keep every wall (nothing comes down) - The rooms are used the same way. The walk-in gets a 3.2 m sneaker wall, entered through Bedroom 2's own door, next to the main bedroom door. - ✅ No permit, no hacking cost, nothing to undo. ⚠️ The display can't be seen from the sofa, and the walk-in isn't joined to the main bedroom.

3. Collector's wing (W3 and W5 come down) - Bedrooms 2 and 3 become one room with a 1.6 m build table in the middle. A see-through sneaker wall splits off a 2.7 m walk-in that opens to the main bedroom. - ✅ Best for building and having friends over. ⚠️ Only one bedroom left, W3 may need a PE, and it's the hardest to undo.

4. Glass-box gallery (W1 and W2 become full-height glass with a glass door; W5 comes down) - The display room becomes a lit showcase you see from the sofa and the corridor. Low cabinets go along the glass, tall ones on the solid walls. - ✅ The most wow. ⚠️ Less wall space for cabinets, glass costs more, and more sunlight reaches the collection.

For the collection, in every layout

  • UV film or blackout blinds on the display room window, because sun yellows LEGO and fades Bearbricks. Light the cabinets with LEDs.
  • Glass-door cabinets. Shelf depths: LEGO 400–450 mm, Bearbrick 250–300 mm, sneakers 350–400 mm.
  • 45–55% humidity in the walk-in and the display room, with a dehumidifier and aircon dry mode. Damp crumbles midsoles and moulds suede and boxes.
  • Glass sliding doors on the open kitchen edge (drawn in every layout). You need them for wok cooking, and they keep grease off the displays.
  • The everyday basics: a shoe bench at the door, free-standing racks in the shelter, and utility storage in the yard. Plan aircon trunking and cabinet power points before the carpentry.

Before any hacking, confirm with HDB or your contractor

  • The permit through an HDB-registered contractor, and whether you need a PE (especially for W3)
  • The block's completion date (all 4 layouts leave the bathrooms alone)
  • Switches, pipes and trunking inside W1, W3 and W5
  • Beams above those walls; a removed wall leaves a bulkhead line on the ceiling
  • Measurements on site. The main bedroom is only about 2.7 m clear, so I've drawn a queen bed; a king would leave under 800 mm at its foot.
