Couple who both work from home

We are
couple, both work from home full-time
Coming up
lots of video calls
Must-haves
two separate offices, a gaming corner

Claude’s answer

Best fit: Layout 1, "Bedroom in the middle". Office A goes in Bedroom 3, Office B and the gaming corner go in the main bedroom, and you sleep in Bedroom 2. With both of you on calls all day, a bedroom between the offices is the cheapest soundproofing there is, and no wall comes down.

How I read your plan (centre-line sizes, so usable space is about 0.1–0.2 m less): - Living/dining: 3.55 × 6.8 m, windows on two sides. - Bedroom 3: 3.15 × 3.7 m. Bedroom 2: 2.9 × 3.7 m. Their doors sit side by side. - Main bedroom: 3.0 × 5.15 m, with Bath 1 en-suite. - Kitchen: 3.9 × 2.6 m, open to the dining.

Wall check

Wall Can it go? Why
S1 No Structural (black)
H1–H4 No Household shelter; small screw fixings only
B1–B4 Not for 3 years after the block's completion, then limited and with a permit Bathroom walls
E1–E14 No External walls, windows, main entrance, A/C ledge
W1–W7 Yes, with HDB approval via a DRC contractor (and a PE if it's concrete) Thin partitions
K1–K2 Already open Kitchen edge, no wall

Walls I'm unsure about: - W1 runs on from the black wall, so it may be concrete. - W3 and W5 may have beams above them. - W6 likely holds the light switches. - W7 may have pipes in it.

To check them, measure the thickness, do a knock test, and look for switches, pipes and beams. The contractor's HDB permit application will confirm it.

Layouts

You need all three rooms, so none of the layouts knocks a wall down.

1. Bedroom in the middle (best fit) - Bedroom 3 becomes Office A, Bedroom 2 a bedroom with a queen bed, and the main bedroom Office B plus a gaming desk for two. - Pros: the desks are about 6 m apart and the offices share no wall. - Cons: you sleep in the smaller room, with Bath 2 at night and a 1.65 m wardrobe.

2. Offices side by side - Offices in Bedrooms 3 and 2, with full-height cabinets on both sides of W3. The main bedroom stays a bedroom, and a PC gaming nook goes on the shelter wall. - Pros: you keep the main bedroom and its en-suite. - Cons: a thin wall and side-by-side doors between the offices, so voices carry.

3. Gaming room for two - Bedroom 3 becomes a gaming room with a sofa bed. Bedroom 2 is Office A, and Office B sits at the main bedroom's side window. - Pros: a full gaming room and a guest bed. - Cons: one of you works in the bedroom.

4. Big-screen gaming lounge - Offices in Bedrooms 3 and 2, the main bedroom stays, and the living room gets a projector wall and a 2-seat gaming recliner. - Pros: you can play together on a big screen. - Cons: the same shared office wall as layout 2, and you'll need blackout blinds.

For the calls: - Fit door seals and drop seals on both office doors. Acoustic panels stop echo, not noise. - Face the window, and keep the gaming kit out of the camera's view. - Run power and LAN before the carpentry.

The calls don't change which room does what, so I planned for them from day one rather than drawing separate "now" and "after" versions.

Also drawn: a shoe cabinet with a seat at the door, the shelter set up as a storeroom with free-standing racks, and a utility cabinet in the yard. Plan the aircon trunking for three rooms that run all day. If you cook with a wok, add glass doors along K1–K2.

Confirm with HDB or your contractor before any hacking

  • Permits: nothing here needs hacking. Removing a W wall later needs an HDB permit, plus a Professional Engineer if the wall is concrete.
  • Completion date: your block's completion date sets the 3-year bathroom rule.
  • Services: switches, pipes and trunking inside any wall you touch.
  • Beams: check above W3 and W5.
  • Measurements: measure on site before you buy furniture.

I read your plan from a photo, so check these sizes against your official HDB floor plan.
